# IMDb Movies and TV Shows — Exploratory Data Analysis

## Project Overview

This project explores a dataset of movies and TV shows using Exploratory Data Analysis (EDA) in Python. The goal is to understand patterns in IMDb ratings, genre distributions, release years, and differences between movies and TV shows.

The analysis also examines missing data, the relationship between IMDb scores and vote counts, and the highest-rated titles in the dataset.

## Tools Used

- Python
- Pandas
- Matplotlib
- Google Colab
- GitHub

## Objectives

The main objectives of this project are to:

Analyze the distribution of IMDb scores.

Compare average ratings between movies and TV shows.

Explore the relationship between IMDb ratings and vote counts.

Identify the most common genres and compare their average ratings.

Examine title release trends over time.

Investigate age certifications and missing data.

Compare average IMDb scores across release decades.

Identify the highest-rated movies and TV shows using a minimum vote threshold.

## Key Findings

### 1. IMDb Ratings
- The average IMDb score across rated titles was approximately 6.53, with a median of 6.6.
- Most titles had ratings concentrated around the 6.5–7.5 range.

### 2. Movies vs. TV Shows
- TV shows had a higher average IMDb score (7.02) than movies (6.27).
- The average rating difference was approximately 0.75 points.

### 3. Ratings and Vote Counts
- IMDb scores and vote counts had a weak positive correlation of approximately 0.19.
- Vote count alone does not explain much of the variation in IMDb ratings.

### 4. Genre Analysis
- Drama and Comedy were the most common genre assignments in the dataset.
- History had the highest average score among the top 10 genres by average rating, at 7.15.
- Genre averages should be interpreted alongside the number of titles in each genre.

### 5. Release Trends
- The number of titles in the dataset peaked in 2019, with 848 titles.
- Movies generally outnumbered TV shows across most years examined.
- Both categories grew toward 2019, followed by a decline in counts.

### 6. Age Certifications and Missing Data
- Approximately 45% of titles had no recorded age certification.
- All missing values in the seasons column belonged to movies, while shows had recorded season counts.
- A total of 523 titles were missing both IMDb scores and vote counts.

### 7. Ratings by Decade
- The 1960s had the highest average score among the decades analyzed (7.34), based on only 11 rated titles.
- The 2020s had an average score of 6.32 in this dataset.
- Differences in sample size and dataset coverage should be considered when interpreting decade-level averages.

### 8. Highest-Rated Titles
- The highest-rated TV show in the filtered list was *Khawatir*, with a score of 9.6.
- The highest-rated movies in the filtered list were *David Attenborough: A Life on Our Planet* and *C/o Kancharapalem*, both scoring 9.0.
- The lists included only titles with at least 1,000 IMDb votes.

- ## Data Cleaning and Preparation

The following data preparation steps were performed during the analysis:

- Checked the dataset for duplicate rows; no full-row duplicates were found.
- Examined missing values across all columns.
- Investigated missing seasons and confirmed that all missing values belonged to movies.
- Created a separate dataset containing titles with available IMDb scores for rating analysis.
- Excluded missing IMDb scores and vote counts from analyses requiring those values.
- Parsed the genres column into individual genre categories for genre-level analysis.
- Applied a minimum threshold of 1,000 IMDb votes when identifying top-rated titles.

- ## Limitations

- The analysis is limited to the titles and release years represented in the dataset.
- Age certification data was missing for approximately 45% of titles.
- Missing IMDb scores and vote counts may affect some comparisons.
- Some release decades contain far fewer titles than others, making their average ratings less stable.
- The 2022 release-year count may reflect incomplete dataset coverage.
- Correlation between IMDb scores and vote counts does not establish causation.
- The results are descriptive and should not be interpreted as proof that one factor causes higher or lower ratings.
